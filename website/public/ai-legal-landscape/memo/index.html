DAUBERT PACKET BUILDER — GLOBAL DECISIONS FROM THE COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Date: 2026-09-21 Inputs: Five parallel research lanes (incumbent legal AI; expert-witness/Daubert tooling; evidence verification & toxicology data; plaintiff/mass-tort startups; funding + Moonshots LIVE). Companions: the landscape in 18 slides and the field guide of real screens. Purpose: Lock the functionality, workflow, evidence-verification standard, platform posture and business model for the TKG legal-lane product before building the version the Sky Valley counsel will use on a new case.

1. The finding that drives everything

Nobody sells this. Across ~60 companies reviewed:

  • Incumbents (Harvey $15.5B, Legora $5.6B, Clio/vLex $5B, Thomson Reuters, LexisNexis) verify the law, not the science. KeyCite, Shepard's Verify, Westlaw's Litigation Document Analyzer and Legora's forthcoming AI-native citator (beta 2026-09-14) all validate legal citations against licensed legal corpora. None retrieves or version-pins IARC / ATSDR / EPA IRIS / cohort literature.
  • Every expert-witness product is procedural and defense-tilted. Daubert Tracker (256,642 case records), Expert Witness Profiler ($35–$625 reports), Lexis Context "Daubert scorecard", Westlaw Profiler, Expert Radar, Pre/Dicta answer "what happened to this expert before". None assesses whether the current opinion's sources are sound.
  • ~$700M of plaintiff-side legal AI (EvenUp $2B+, Eve $1B+, Supio, Darrow, Finch) stops at the demand letter or at "exhibit assembly". Supio explicitly does not address Daubert or scientific literature.
  • The two scientifically serious neighbors are not tools. Justpoint ($95M, causation inference to originate its own cases) and Parambil ($6M seed, medical-record review with clinical-guideline retrieval). Neither does general-causation literature tiering, version pinning, both-sides, or an expert-reliability dossier.
  • Evidence verification in the market stops at Layer 1–2 (existence, good-law/retraction). Only Clearbrief (Dec 2025) and Filevine LOIS (Sep 2, 2026) attempt proposition support, both for case law only, both admitting human review is required. scite/Consensus are opaque classifiers or vote counts — not court-grade.

Positioning sentence: They verify the law. We verify the science.

2. Decisions

D1 — Product posture: an evidence layer, not another legal-AI chat

Ship the packet as an exportable exhibit object + MCP server that plugs into the tools the firm already pays for (Word, CoCounsel, Harvey, Everlaw, Supio, Eve, Filevine, Litify, SmartAdvocate). Do not compete for the research seat. Rationale: 99% of PI attorneys won't use unverifiable AI output (Supio/Thirdside, Jul 2026, n=207); firms already juggle ~18 live AI tools (Activant, Jun 2026); Harvey and Everlaw already consume external evidence via MCP; SmartAdvocate shipped an MCP server Aug 2026.

D2 — The verification standard (non-negotiable, and the moat)

Every claim in a packet is: 1. Anchored to a version-pinned source — edition/volume/assessment date + DOI/PMID + Perma/WARC capture + content hash (IARC Monograph vol./year; ATSDR ToxProfile edition; IRIS assessment date; CompTox dataset version). 2. Passage-quoted — page/section/paragraph locator and verbatim text for every support and contradiction link (this is what separated Ferlito (admitted) from Kohls (excluded)). 3. Human-adjudicated for strength — T1/T2/T3 tier keyed to source class, with the adjudicator's identity and date recorded. Regulatory classification (IARC Group, IRIS RfD) is labeled separately from causation-at-dose evidence. 4. Carrying a mandatory contrary-evidence record — both sides of every contested claim, including confounders and the authors' own caveats (directly answers the "cherry-picking" grounds in the Acetaminophen and Baby Food MDL exclusions). 5. Re-checked at build time — existence + Crossref/Retraction Watch status for the source and its own reference list (1 in 277 new papers now contains a fabricated citation — Lancet, May 2026). 6. Shipped with a machine-readable, signed Evidence Verification Report (JSON-LD + manifest) that opposing counsel or chambers can re-run.

Reuse, don't rebuild: DOI/PMID/Crossref/Europe PMC resolution, Retraction Watch feed, Semantic Scholar/scite as candidate generators only, CompTox CTX + NTP NICE APIs, Perma.cc, and existing case-law cite-checkers.

D3 — Provenance & disclosure log is a first-class export

After Conservation Law Foundation v. Shell (D. Conn. May 18, 2026) compelled production of an expert's AI prompts, and with proposed FRE 707 subjecting machine-generated evidence to Rule 702 scrutiny, the packet must carry an audit log of every source, version, human decision and generation step, plus a certification paragraph mapped to the judge's standing order (143 federal standing orders as of May 2026). Build the log first; it turns a liability into a feature.

D4 — Structure the packet the way judges already read

Organize exhibits around the Reference Manual on Scientific Evidence, 4th ed. (FJC/NAS, Dec 31, 2025 — free, no software wrapper exists) and the Rule 702 factors under the 2023 amendment's preponderance standard. The packet must demonstrate the ten things 2023–2026 exclusions demanded: realistic dose reconstruction; exposure–endpoint fidelity; systematic (not selective) literature review; named confounders; transparent Bradford Hill with temporality; plaintiff-specific differential etiology; independent (not counsel-built) method; everything disclosed in the report; tool/input provenance; regulatory-vs-causal distinction.

D5 — Dual standard output: Frye and Rule 702

Washington (Erickson) is Frye; federal MDLs are amended-702. Every packet renders both views and flags the delta. Build for the strict federal version. Note: cert in Union Carbide v. Sommerville (No. 25-919) was denied May 26, 2026, so the Fourth Circuit's softer weight-not-admissibility rule stands and the circuit split persists; the packet should flag the governing circuit.

D6 — Two buyers, one packet

Sell to the firm (per matter) and to the testifying toxicologist/epidemiologist (per expert credential + prior-testimony pack). Nobody sells to the expert today. Prior-testimony data is licensed in (Daubert Tracker, Exlitem, Steno/Lexitas transcripts), never rebuilt.

D7 — Business model

  • Per-matter packet fee to the firm — it is approved as expert/consulting cost, not a software line item. Anchor against the human alternative (a defense-side ToxStrategies engagement is six figures [UNVERIFIED]) and above the EWP ladder ($35–$625 for procedural reports).
  • Expert seat for the credential/testimony pack.
  • Licensing out the curated, versioned claim layer to AI/legal/clinical platforms (Wiley: $49M FY26 AI licensing; OpenEvidence reached $12B by licensing NEJM/JAMA in). Curation density, not volume, is what gets paid for.
  • Outcome-based pricing is absent across all surveyed legal AI — leave as an option for litigation funders (Legalist $415M fund) who need to price Daubert risk.

D8 — Beachhead

PCB / Erickson v. Monsanto (Sky Valley; $185M reinstated by the Washington Supreme Court Oct 30, 2025; ~$1.74B+ in SVEC verdicts) plus one federal MDL where causation is now the whole game after Monsanto v. Durnell (SCOTUS, Jun 25, 2026, FIFRA preemption of failure-to-warn): PFAS/AFFF (15,222 pending) or Roundup. 198,825 actions pending across 158 MDLs (Apr 2026); talc, PFAS, paraquat and Roundup are all toxicology-causation fights.

D9 — Revised counsel workflow (was Frame → Assemble → Argue → Witness → File)

  1. Frame — theory of harm; substance(s); exposure pathway; endpoints; jurisdiction (Frye/702 toggle).
  2. Reconstruct — dose/exposure model with plaintiff-specific facts; flag counsel-supplied assumptions (the Baby Food "black box" failure).
  3. Assemble — claim set from the TKG graph; T1/T2/T3 badges; version pins; retraction re-check.
  4. Contest — for every contested claim, the defense's likely attack (Bradford Hill gaps, confounders, animal-to-human extrapolation, regulatory-vs-causal) and the counter-evidence, side by side.
  5. Witness — expert credential + prior-testimony pack (licensed data), reliance-materials list, consistency check against the expert's own publications.
  6. Certify — provenance log, standing-order certification paragraph, Evidence Verification Report.
  7. File — chambers-ready PDF with exhibit tabs, citation appendix, version manifest; plus JSON-LD/MCP export into the firm's suite.

D10 — Competitive advantage that is real, not claimed

  1. A named expert who has already been cross-examined curating the corpus (a database can be copied; a witness record cannot).
  2. Claim-level provenance nobody else has productized for science.
  3. Both-sides neutrality — the only packet a judge's chambers would accept as an aid rather than advocacy.
  4. Expert-correction feedback loop — exactly the data moat investors say they fund (Activant, Jun 2026: "value accrues to whoever owns what a model cannot manufacture: … a proprietary data asset").
  5. MCP-native distribution into every suite in the plaintiff stack.
  6. A won proof case with a public appellate record.

3. Risks and fast followers to watch

  • TR / LexisNexis bolting a scientific corpus onto Profiler / Context — they own the expert analytics and the citator.
  • Legora's editorially governed corpus + AI-native citator (GA Q4 2026) is the most extensible incumbent architecture.
  • CaseMark adding a "defend my expert" workflow (today it's a text-critique aimed at attacking experts).
  • Parambil adding a literature layer — approach as a build-with partner first.
  • Justpoint productizing its causation engine.
  • Sommerville split now persists (cert denied May 26, 2026): 4th Cir. matters are softer; do not assume one federal bar.
  • Proposed FRE 707 — build the audit trail before it lands.

4. Partners and channels (not competitors)

SmartAdvocate (MCP server, 20k users) → Filevine (~6,000 firms, partner directory) → Litify (embeds Supio already) → Supio (exhibit assembly, TR Ventures) → Eve (1,400 firms) → Parambil → Daubert Tracker / Exlitem / Courtroom Insight (expert history) → Steno / Lexitas / Verbit (transcripts) → Legalist / Mustang (funder underwriting) → AAJ, MassTortNexus (where the buyers gather).

5. Knowledge Gardens business-plan implications

  • Lead with "the ground truth AI needs to survive" and the expert-correction loop; do not lead with "AI for lawyers".
  • Every winner in the "one architecture, many verticals" set went deep first (OpenEvidence $12B single-vertical; Harvey deep-then-adjacent). TKG is the deep one; the other gardens are proof of pattern, said in one breath.
  • Harvey ($550M raise, Sep 9, 2026) is now shipping its own model and benchmark — moving down toward ground truth. Line: "They're moving toward ground truth. We are the ground truth."
  • Curated-science licensing to AI is real, recurring revenue (Wiley $49M FY26) and pays for scarcity — a versioned toxicology claim layer is scarce; raw ATSDR PDFs are not.
